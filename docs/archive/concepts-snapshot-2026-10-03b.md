# How it works – concept snapshot 2, 3 Oct 2026 (after re-checking the first 8 upset races)

Written at Eddie's request (3 Oct 13:49): "an updated version of How It Works for my own reference".
Built from the full re-check of T33, T32, R21, R29, R9, R30, R12, R39 – every check run on every race, minute by minute through the race (details and raw numbers in blank-sheet-reads.md). The results are known, so this is a working picture of what we SEE, not a tested rule. It replaces the first snapshot where they differ.

## The basic idea (unchanged)
- Every horse and every jockey carries a fixed IMPRINT – the distances between its natal bodies at birth (natal–natal = ability).
- The sky at the race carries the ENERGY of the moment – the distances between the bodies now. A few stand out as the race's KEY NUMBERS.
- A winner shows where imprint and moment meet – on one body, at the race time.

## Step 1 – the race's key numbers
Look at the sky's strongest pairs (the top 12) and its BUSIEST body (the body in the most strong pairs).
The note played loudest is:
- a number the sky says twice (the REPEATED number – also across RA and Dec, which is the same distance), or
- the same number with the decimal moved (4.444 and 44.44; 11.11 and 111.1), or
- the number of the busiest body's pair.

```
R29:  #5 Pallas/Sedna  14.4407  ┐
      #11 Altair/Ceres 14.4496  ┘  130/9 twice

R39:  #2 Chiron/Jupiter  RA 12.2226  ┐
      #6 Bellatrix/Mars Dec 12.2211  ┘  110/9 twice, across RA and Dec
```

## Step 2 – ONE body that carries it, steady, in the runner's own charts
Look in the horse natal, the jockey natal and horse × jockey for a distance equal to a key number.
- It must sit on a body that does not move over the unknown birth hour. Matches on the Moon, Sun, Mercury, Venus or Mars may not exist at the real birth time – the beaten favourites' matches are very often on these.
- Best when the SAME body carries it in more than one place (horse, jockey, between them).

## Step 3 – the sky reaches that SAME body, in BOTH charts, at the race time
This is the part the minute-by-minute re-check brought out. The winner's key body is reached by the sky in the horse's chart AND the jockey's chart, during the race:

- A contact counts as "at the race time" when it peaks at the off, or is applying at the off and peaks in the following minutes while the race is still being run (flat ~1 min, jumps up to ~12 min).
- A peak is simply higher than the minute before and after – no minimum drop.
- Sun → Makemake on both charts: R9 Arvico Bleu 25/1, and earlier Perfect Part 125/1 (Sun/Makemake on both charts, the only one in its race).

## The closed loop – when it counts
The strongest form: a key pair LANDS on the very body that carries that pair's number.
```
                 NATAL JUNO (Forget You Not, R12)
                          ▲
   #10 royal Regulus/Transpluto (φ 1.6184) lands on it, both legs applying
   horse Juno–Regulus 1.6193 · horse × jockey 1.6187 · jockey ×10 16.187
   + Mars reaches the jockey's Juno in the first minute of the race
```
- It counts when it is at FULL SCALE in the pair's own coordinate (T33, R12 – both winners).
- "Loops" at another decimal place (×10, ÷10) or in the pair's second coordinate turned up on beaten runners (R9 Slanelough 3rd, R12 Jarlath 2nd / Pens Man 4th, R21 Regional 4th).
- Not every winner has one (T32, R29, R9, R30, R39 had none) – the both-charts body at the race time is the more common shape.

## What does NOT decide it on its own
- Counts of anything – landings, contacts, peaks, royal stars, hubs, groups, half numbers, midpoints. ("General numbers don't mean anything.")
- A body carrying numbers in three places (Fiamette R29 4th, Reserve Tank R30 last).
- An exact peak at the off by itself (beaten runners often have more – Mondammej R21, Capuchinero T33, Jarlath R12).
- The "busiest pair held alone" line – it fired on beaten favourites (R29, R39).
- Same body on both charts reached by the moment – the favourite showed it too in R30 (Ballyblack, Makemake, 3rd). What made the winner different there: its body (Quaoar) belonged to the #1 pair and took BOTH legs of the busiest pair.

## In one sentence
> The winner is the runner whose ONE steady body holds the sky's key number, in both horse and jockey, and the sky reaches that same body in both charts at the race time – exactly at the off or building through the first minutes of the race.
