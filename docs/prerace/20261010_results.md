## Same-body distance ratios: a simpler look (Eddie, 11 Oct). CORRECTED the same day
**What is measured** (`tools/samebody_ratios.py`):
1. For one chart and one body X, the same-body distance is the distance from where X is in the race sky now (topocentric at the course, from the minute sky file, interpolated every 2 s) to where X was in that chart at 12:00 on the birth day (the POS file).
   - Bodies: Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto, Chiron, Ceres, Pallas, Juno, Vesta, Rahu, Eris, Sedna, Haumea, Makemake, Gonggong, Quaoar, Orcus, Transpluto.
   - Left out: the Moon, and Ketu (its distance always equals Rahu's).
2. Three measures:
   - **RA** = |ΔRA| (wrapped to ±180°);
   - **Dec** = |ΔDec|;
   - **Flat** (direct) = √(ΔRA² + ΔDec²), with no cos Dec.
   
   The Sky (great-circle) version is kept separately for comparison.
3. A ratio is the larger distance divided by the smaller, for two same-body distances in the same measure. They can be two bodies of one chart, or a horse body : a jockey body (across the pair).
4. The ratio is compared with the interval list (lattice/families.py: whole numbers 1–9, the small fractions, √2 forms, φ forms).
   - It is kept when it is within 0.005% of a value in the real race window (off to finish).
   - **X** = it comes exact inside the race.
   - **held** = within 0.005% at both the off and the finish.
   - **X,held** = both. The first run showed these as 'held' only, which hid some X; the checker found this on Reserve Tank, and the tool is fixed.
   - Distances under 0.05° are skipped.
