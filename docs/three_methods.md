# Three ways of reading a race — 6 Oct 2026

Written at Eddie's request (6 Oct 22:13) to fix the three methods before Carlisle. Eddie said "not in a strictly defined way", so these are ways of looking rather than rules. The order of steps is a guide. We still just say what we see, one race at a time, layer by layer.

The three methods come from nine races read with the results known: Catterick, Ffos Las, Newcastle, Doncaster, Carlisle, Wincanton and Exeter, plus the second looks at Newcastle and Wincanton and a quick look at York. The detail is in sky-dig §46–§70 and the race summaries.

## The working process, for all three methods
Added 7 Oct 2026 at Eddie's request (08:44): "we have been very successful in these race reads because we have gone one body at a time, methodically, and say what we see with that body, then pull it all together as we go & at the end. This is the working process we need to implement for each method. One at a time, even one body per one layer at a time; that is how you consistently see all the details."

- **One body at a time.** For each body, say what we see with that body, then stop before moving on.
- **One body per layer** where a method has layers. In Method 2, for example: Layer 1 with Rahu, then Layer 1 with Ketu, then L2 with Uranus, and so on. The Moon is taken on its own as the clock.
- **Pull it together as we go.** After each body, note where it lands on something already seen: the same string, the same natal body, a stack forming, the same star coming back.
- **Pull it all together at the end:**
  - first within each method;
  - then across the three methods, looking for the string or body where they meet.
- **Why it works:** on Exeter the first read (the whole race at once) looked weak. Read body by body, it showed the following, and several only appeared because a later body landed on a string an earlier one had already used:
  - stacks on the jockey;
  - the horse and jockey together on single strings;
  - one string where all three methods meet: Aldebaran–Procyon, Transpluto holding, the Moon striking in the race.

How each method takes it:
- **Method 1:** one natal body at a time, horse first, then jockey. For each body: what it makes inside its own chart, and with the other chart. Then the short list of bodies that matter.
- **Method 2:** one body within one layer at a time. For each: who is tuned in and in UNISON, and the timing around the off.
- **Method 3:** one sky body at a time, in the order listed below.

## Method 1 — THE IMPRINT (what the charts are made of)
**What it answers:** which of this horse's and this jockey's bodies matter.

**How it goes:**
- Read the horse natal, the jockey natal, and horse × jockey.
- Look for:
  - the busiest body;
  - the body that receives several pairs (the receiver);
  - pairs that land on a body alone;
  - the race's repeated numbers sitting on a steady body (for example 23√2, 140/9, 10φ).
- Steady bodies only: no Moon, Sun, Mercury or Venus as carriers, because those move over the unknown birth hour.
- The result is a short list of bodies per chart. Examples: Newcastle, the jockey's Orcus and the horse's Gonggong; Wincanton, the horse's Quaoar and Makemake and the jockey's Orcus.

**Background:** blank-sheet design and reads; concept snapshots 1 and 2.

## Method 2 — THE TUNED-IN LAYERS (what strikes, and when)
**What it answers:** what the sky plays around the off and during the race, and who is tuned to it. **The Moon is the clock.**

**How it goes:**
- Go layer by layer: Layer 1 star bases → Nodes → L2 slow → L3 medium → L4 Sun/Mercury/Venus/Moon → L5 fast points (colour only).
- For each sky chord live in the window (about 30 min before the off to just after the finish), note:
  - which charts make a chord on the same base (**tuned in**);
  - which make the same chord (**UNISON**);
  - which have the same three points (**full triangle**).
- Mark the Moon's crossings of natal Decs. When the Moon sits on a natal body's Dec, that body is in UNISON on every Dec chord the Moon makes.
- **Meeting points:** where a tuned chord lands on a Method-1 body.
- **The 'against' check**, run on every runner:
  - discord (√2);
  - same note (who holds it tighter);
  - in/out.
  - Eddie: "some things we may rethink like discords". Discord does not separate in 6-runner fields.

**Background:** concept snapshot 3; the race summaries.

## Method 3 — THE SLOW-BODY READ (who is being held, and how)
**What it answers:** which natal bodies the slower sky is holding, how firmly, and in what shape.

**How it goes:**
- **Take one sky body at a time:** Sun, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Ceres, Pallas, Juno, Vesta, Uranus, Neptune, Pluto, Chiron, Eris, Sedna, Haumea, Makemake, Quaoar, Orcus, Gonggong, Transpluto, then the nodes. The Moon is left out here because it is the clock.
- **List all its chords with the stars at the off.** For each chord, note the base, the three distances, their families, and the exact time.
- **Note who is tightest or in UNISON on each.** Then, for the winning pair's holdings, write out the actual distances on both sides.
- **Where the points sit on the string:**
  - inside it;
  - beyond one end;
  - a mirror, with the sky body beyond one end and the natal body beyond the other, or both the same distance from one star on opposite sides;
  - or the same body on both sides (sky Neptune with natal Neptune).
- **Movement through the race:** check the deviation at off−30, the off, the finish and off+30. Is the chord applying, exact in the race, or separating? Slow chords that are not exact on the day still count. Eddie: "more slow may mean more strong".
- **Slow parallels:** a sky body sitting on a natal Dec. That natal body then joins every Dec chord the sky body makes (the slow form of a Moon crossing).
- **Stacks:** several sky bodies on ONE string, all holding the SAME natal body. Eddie: "this type of occurrence is very significant — the combinations".
- **The recurring star:** one star running through the winners' strings. Procyon at Newcastle, Rigel at Wincanton.
- **Then two checks:**
  - **Number check** on each sky body: its Dec and its distances to stars against kφ, φⁿ, k√2, whole numbers and ninths, within ±0.002°. Chance is about 0.44 φ/√2/whole hits and 1.5 ninths per body.
  - A body's own RA does not count as a number (Eddie, 7 Oct): RA has no fixed absolute start, unlike Dec from the equator. RA distances between points still count.
  - Natal to transit, same body (7 Oct): ±0.002 is a hit; ±0.002–0.005 is shown as "near" and counted only when it joins something else. (At ±0.01 nearly every body hits a ninth by chance.)
  - **Power points** (the §42 star-lattice nodes): is any sky body or natal point on one?

**Background:** sky-dig §67 (Newcastle), §68 (York), §69 (power points), §70 (Wincanton), §71 (Carlisle), §72 (Exeter).

## How the three link
```
METHOD 1  names the bodies          →  "which bodies matter"     (the imprint)
METHOD 3  shows the slow sky        →  "who is held"             (the strings, the stacks)
          holding those bodies
METHOD 2  shows the clock striking  →  "when"                    (the Moon, the off)
```
The strongest picture is when all three point to the SAME body. Wincanton is the clearest so far:
```
METHOD 1   River Bray's QUAOAR is the #1 imprint body
METHOD 3   Mercury holds his Quaoar on Alkaid–Altair, applying through the race
METHOD 2   the Moon makes his Quaoar's own chord on that string at 14:24:30 (UNISON, only chart),
           then reaches his Quaoar Dec 1 min after the finish
```
Other examples:
- **Newcastle:** the jockey's Orcus (Method 1) is held by Neptune as a mirror on Procyon–Rigel, with Eris at the middle (Method 3). The Moon does not strike it; the Moon is at its Dec limit.
- **Wincanton:** the jockey's Makemake (Method 1) is held by the Sun 1:6:7, exact in the race, together with sky Makemake. That is a stack, and Methods 2 and 3 meet on it.

## Lessons from the special reads — Dettori, Messi, the lottery winner, Frankel (7 Oct 2026)
Added at Eddie's request (22:18): "yes, you can now — remember we are still SAY WHAT YOU SEE". These are things to look out for, each with where we saw it — not rules or scores. Detail in sky-dig §73–§76 and the summary docs.

**Birth times (Eddie, 22:17):** all horse and jockey charts are read at midday (12:00). No discounting of findings for the unknown birth hour.

### In the natal chart (Method 1)
- **Out-of-bounds bodies.** Frankel's Mars (+26.508, 3.07° out) was his main body. Mars was also out of bounds for Queally, the lottery winner and Diffident; Messi's Moon was far out (4.60°).
- **Bodies stationary or nearly so on the birth day.** Frankel's Mars and Pluto were almost stationary in Dec, so their figures held all day.
- **Whole-number lattices in Dec**, between bodies and stars (Dec only — RA has no fixed zero):
  - Frankel: Mars–Aldebaran–Orcus (10, 22, 32) and Jupiter–Uranus–Haumea (17, 25).
  - The lottery winner: the Transpluto lattice (5, 25).
  - Messi: the Venus Dec line, struck by the match sky at fractions of Venus's Dec.
- **Midpoints between bodies.** Frankel: Mars at the RA midpoint of Eris and Neptune; Orcus at the Dec midpoint of Pluto and Sedna.
- **Strings held by two or three natal bodies.** Frankel's Antares–Spica; Dettori's Antares–Rigel.

### In the race sky (Method 3)
- **Slow bodies sitting on a key body's strings through the event.** Saturn on Messi's Sedna; Uranus, Vesta and Saturn on Frankel's Mars, each on a different string.
- **Fast bodies striking key strings in the last minutes before the off.** Dettori: Mars and the Sun within 2 minutes. Frankel: Mercury at 3 minutes, the Moon at 21 seconds. Lottery: the Sun at the draw.
  - Chance: the Moon touches a natal string about every 2 minutes, Mercury every 20–40 minutes.
- **One star reached by number from several sky bodies.** Castor at Frankel's race; Regulus through Messi's goals; Algol in the lottery chart.
- **Natal-to-transit numbers for the slow bodies.** Eris gave numbers for Messi, Dettori and the lottery winner, not for Frankel; Frankel's Quaoar (2/9) and Transpluto (8/9) did.
- **Features of the day.** Frankel's race: New Moon day, the Sun at its highest Dec, Mercury out of bounds.
- **Easy stars.** When sky bodies sit next to a star, chords on it come easily (Pleiades and Aldebaran at Frankel's race).

### In the tuned layers (Method 2)
- **Bases between two bodies can show Moon strikes that Method 3 misses.** Frankel: the Moon on Mars's base (Uranus–Castor) 15 s after the off and on Jupiter/Juno's base (Sedna–Fomalhaut) 8 s before the finish.
- **Swaps in the nodes layer.** Frankel: sky Mars on natal Uranus's node base, sky Uranus on natal Saturn's.

### Across the methods
- All three met on one body, Frankel's Mars: out of bounds with its lattice (M1), held by three slow bodies (M3), its base struck by the Moon in the race (M2).
- All four were read with the result known, and only the winners' charts were read.

### Method 1 lists for prediction (Eddie, 8 Oct 07:32–07:34)
- Idea: a Method 1 list for each horse, one for each jockey, and a combined horse × jockey list (shared strings, same-body pairs, cross-chart parallels and numbers, cross-chart figures). Lists are reusable — a natal chart doesn't change between races.
- **Keep the lists WIDE for now, not trimmed** (Eddie: "remember we need to keep things wide and SAY WHAT WE SEE AT THE MOMENT because often it is the combination that is important rather than an individual hit").

### Counts are background; the reading is in the texture (Eddie, 8 Oct 17:06)
"there is a lot of texture ... that you reduce to counts". Counting chords per chart (tightest, strings played, strikes) flattens what matters: one string played by several bodies in sequence; mirrors and midpoints; exclusivity (a string only this chart holds); the same chord in the horse's and jockey's charts on one string; Method 1 links (parallels, shared strings) showing on a sky string; a whole natal figure held; a hub played by the Moon; the order of strikes through the race. Comparisons between runners are made in these terms, side by side, not by totals.

### The two lights (Eddie, 8 Oct 17:24)
"the moon is the clock and the sun is equally important — just those 2 in that way". The Moon and the Sun are read first and in full for every race and every runner: their chords and holds through the window, the strings where one is a base end with the other (the Sun's own distances played by the Moon — e.g. Doncaster Sun–Betelgeuse, Sun–Bellatrix), same-body chords, natal Sun → sky Sun and natal Moon → sky Moon numbers, and where the two play the same string. No other body has this role.

### Reading every runner — the procedure (Eddie, 8 Oct 17:38–17:41)
For every runner in a race (horse, then jockey), with the same settings: natal at 12:00; window off−30 to finish+30; chords ≤0.15%; numbers ±0.002°.
1. **Body by body, every natal body including the natal Sun (natal Moon left out — it moves too far in a day):**
   - Method 1 — what the body is in the chart (strings, numbers, figures, out of bounds / stationary, links to the partner's chart);
   - Method 3 — which sky bodies hold its strings and how (position on the string, stacks, sequences, timing), every sky body except the transit Sun and Moon;
   - Method 2 — the tuned layers on that body (who else is tuned, UNISON, same body).
2. **Then the transit Sun** across all the runner's natal bodies (chords and holds, the strings where it is a base end, same-body chords, natal Sun → sky Sun).
3. **Then the transit Moon** the same way, as the clock; and where the Sun and the Moon play the same string.
4. Then the pair together (shared strings, direct links, same-body chords).
Each runner's reading is kept as a record that keeps the texture, so runners can be set side by side.
